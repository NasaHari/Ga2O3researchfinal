## 1. What the user-specified avalanche diagnostic shows

A candidate *simulated avalanche-like knee* must show a fast rise in accepted terminal current, stay up on the archived branch, and become hole-majority. Use saved spatial current and `ImpactIonization` to localize it when the evidence allows. No substitute breakdown criterion is used here. A hole-majority terminal branch diagnoses the simulated event; it does not identify the initiating carrier or experimentally validate the avalanche coefficients. An endpoint hotspot localizes the solved high-current state, not necessarily the first point of onset.

**Calculation.** Read original DF-ISE `.plt` by named columns in saved acquisition order. `J = |cathode TotalCurrent| / Aref`, with `Aref = π(52.5 × 10⁻⁴ cm)²`; `V = cathode InnerVoltage − anode OuterVoltage` on the reverse branch. The hole fraction is `−sum(anode* hCurrent) / cathode TotalCurrent`, summing the distinct equipotential contacts with their signs, not magnitudes. An apparent fraction slightly over 100% is possible when the signed electron component opposes the net current; it is not a conservation violation. At the high bracket samples the relative terminal current imbalance was below `5 × 10⁻¹⁵` in all selected cases. Count the crossing sample and all later records; the accepted crossing requires five more samples at/above target. Interpolation gives the reported voltage; the sample spacing and the carrier fractions on each side are the direct evidence. “No return” means only up to the final stored bias. Each row below had **zero** later returns below its stated target.

## 3. Technical strengths and parameter audit

- **Current normalization and extraction:** axial symmetry is explicit (`Cylindrical(xAxis=0)`), cathode current accounts for all tied contacts, and common Aref makes the engineering current specification uniform. `I(1 mA/cm²)=86.59 nA`, whereas solver `BreakCriteria` is 1 mA total (~11.55 A/cm²). The cathode internal correction is typically millivolts near a finely bracketed 1 mA/cm² crossing, but about 10 V by compliance in terminated cases. Fig. 5's biases are external saved states, not threshold samples.
- **Fair core comparison:** byte equality of the nominal plated PAR and each architecture's respective temperature-series mesh supports meaningful within-family T comparisons. The nominal plated room-temperature comparisons use shared drift and JTE recipes and same extraction. Mesa and trench simultaneously differ in geometry and spacer/field distribution, so a bare or trench contrast is not an isolated single-feature experiment. R_on,sp normalized to common Aref is a terminal comparison, not a measurement of drift mobility or a correction for real exposed area.
- **Geometry/contact implementation:** mesa is raised above the wafer, trench is recessed, plate is a defined metal-potential boundary, and p-GaN and Schottky contacts are distinct. Current collection is internally coherent across the accepted sweeps. The Schottky sidewall/fillet should not be counted automatically as additional flat semiconductor area. Treat the prescribed p-GaN contact as ideal and keep the boundary interpretation separate from fabrication.
- **Doping and depletion:** drift `2.0e16` Si, substrate `3.6e18` Sn; incomplete-ionization energies 0.036 eV Si, 0.060 eV Sn and nominally 0.20 eV Mg with concentration coefficient `8e-9 eV·cm`, degeneracy 4. With the model's εr=12.4, a planar one-sided 10-µm fully ionized uniform drift estimate gives `q N_D d/ε≈2.92 MV/cm` and `q N_D d²/(2ε)≈1.46 kV`. That is a depletion scale, not a breakdown limit, especially after punch-through or at lateral junctions. Nominal chemical sheet densities `7.5/4.2/2.4e13 cm⁻²` are not equilibrium ionized sheet densities. The reduced-BV cases' endpoint generation relocation from the middle to the outer JTE fits a charge-balance hypothesis, not a proven matched-bias depletion profile.
- **Schottky transport:** nominal 5.10 eV work function minus 4.00-eV model affinity gives 1.10 eV input barrier; sweep uses 0.75–1.50 eV input barriers, not independently measured effective barriers. Global `g=1.56e-8`, `mt=0.38m₀`, 15-nm directed NLM path on Schottky `anode`; GaN local `g=1e-13`, masses `0.25/0.5 m₀` are scoped differently and are not proof of a separate active GaN tunneling path. Image-force lowering `a1=0.72e-4`, `p1=0.5` is an effective PAR setting, not independently validated by the bulk ε. Do not conflate `eDOSMass=0.274` with tunneling mass. The bare rim has Schottky and barrier-lowering definitions, but `NLM_Anode` is seeded from `anode`, not a separate rim NLM; qualify carryover of a nominally identical contact calibration to differently tagged corners.
- **Material law sensitivity:** active GaN `ae,ah = 2.90e8,5.41e6 cm⁻¹`, `be,bh=3.40e7,1.96e7 V/cm`, phonon energy 0.066 eV; oxide-semiconductor block parameters in T02 with phonon energy 0.063 eV. As a sensitivity illustration only, at a *model driving field* of 5 MV/cm and γ=1, the active GaN law gives `αe≈3.23e5`, `αh≈1.07e5 cm⁻¹`; using the coefficients recorded in `PARAMETER_DECISIONS.md` gives `5.09e5` and `3.85e5 cm⁻¹`. This comparison is not a solved field or recommendation to substitute parameters. Cao et al. (APL 112, 262103, 2018, DOI `10.1063/1.5031785`, Eq. 3) studied c-axis transport in epitaxial devices on native bulk GaN, extracting coefficients over 2–3.7 MV/cm. The 5 MV/cm illustration extrapolates their fit and shows parameter sensitivity only. Literature cross-check: Cao et al. also reproduce the exact active electron pair from Kunihiro et al. (IEEE EDL 20, 608–610, 1999, DOI `10.1109/55.806100`). Li, Pala and Chow (JJAP 52, 08JN05, 2013, DOI `10.7567/JJAP.52.08JN05`, Table I, author-posted full text) use the exact active hole pair in a GaN homojunction simulation, but a slightly different electron pair (`2.81e8`, `3.43e7`). These are identifiable precedents, not proof of the project's exact parameter lineage or an independent calibration for the proposed p-GaN/oxide stack.
- **Scope of external evidence:** Yang [1] confirms the experimental distribution and mean for 105-µm devices (DOI `10.1063/1.4983203`, institutional publication abstract); the study's project digitization is not original low-bias instrument data. Ghosh & Singisetti [8] (author-hosted publisher article, Table I) and Fiedler [9] (original article) support directional values, not every material assumption. Cao et al. was used as an applicability cross-check, not verification of a fitted coefficient set in the manuscript. Sentaurus Device User Guide V-2024.03, chapter “Avalanche Generation,” printed pp. 516–517 (local manual), supports naming the numerical discretization settings; existing activation is established primarily by the project decks/logs.

## 4. Reproducibility and correction priorities

**Numerical extraction is reproducible from archived output.** Shared plated material PAR hash prefix `4290929042`; nominal mesh prefixes planar `b5bc26b604`, mesa `63c530a899`, trench `5aeb1272af` recur in the respective 350/400 K decks. The selected bare forward/reverse mesh prefix is `4f7b9d57c846`. Bare, nominal planar/mesa, thin low-barrier and the six elevated-temperature reverse logs include normal closing banners; nominal trench's local case lacks an exact run log, but the closed terminal curve pairs with its final TDR and uses the same packaged nominal PAR. Later Newton tails in dose/thickness runs are flagged above. This is not an independent solver rerun or complete hardware calibration. For endpoint diagnostics use the stated TDR paths, contact voltage, region metadata and mesh weighting; for J–V diagnostics use the original PLT and the formulas above.

**Critical unresolved physics questions:** (1) Is the active GaN multiplication model valid at the simulated stress levels in the intended p-GaN process? (2) How much of the fitted low-barrier/high-temperature leakage is associated with thermionic emission, field-assisted Schottky tunneling or unmodelled interface/hardware paths? (3) Does the location of the stored post-knee hotspot coincide with the first accepted hole-majority state when a bias-matched spatial state is available? (4) How strongly do ideal p-GaN contacts and assumed fixed charge set the modeled dose/thickness sensitivity? The archive establishes no material-limit answer to these questions.

**Prioritized corrections, no deck edits or new runs required for this audit:**
1. Tie avalanche-like labels to the sustained current rises and carrier fractions above. Separate 400 K planar/mesa 1 mA/cm² leakage thresholds and the thin device's 0.1 mA/cm² leakage threshold from the hole-majority high branch. Use the measured brackets rather than asserting a resolved 6-V ranking.
2. Document the origin, scope and limitations of the GaN and β-Ga₂O₃ multiplication and contact-leakage parameter sets; keep the baseline unchanged. Report the nominal JTE/outer trench ring as endpoint localization, not a uniquely proved onset site.
3. Reconcile the flat exposed-area numbers and bare-match wording. Maintain Aref for the operational comparison; state the real differences in contact configuration, numerical setup and interface model.
4. Attach the accepted raw carrier brackets and state bias to each mechanism claim; separate later Newton tails and endpoint model limitations from the validity of the earlier archived current rise.
