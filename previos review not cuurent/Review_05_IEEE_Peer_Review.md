# Review 05 — Independent IEEE Conference Peer Review

**Manuscript:** *Geometry and p-GaN Design Sensitivity in β-Ga₂O₃ Rectifiers: An Axisymmetric TCAD Study*, `Paperwriting/Manuscript_v5_reverse.pdf`, 8 pages, SHA-256 `54ed7649ca1ed2ea01df740e7955d3e6b122ab81b576ba44c190ff00e72720ef`.

**Recommendation: Major Revision.** This is a viable simulation-based device conference paper, but the submitted version omits a completed run that materially changes its thickness story, and its most distinctive result—the separation between an early electron-leakage threshold and a later hole-majority rise—is not evidenced in the paper as directly as the archive allows. The essential fixes are feasible with existing files. This is a technical publication-readiness judgment, not a claim about a particular conference’s page limit, track fit, or acceptance probability.

**Review basis:** Read the complete PDF and checked its rendered figures/tables, the three previous reviews, and selected original `.plt`, `.cmd`, `.par`, `.scm`, solver logs, TDRs and primary literature. The three previously set-aside archive gaps (no-field-plate raw runs, the Fig. 1 refinement runs, and historical Fig. 8 cutline exports) are not reopened or treated as submission blockers. The review still assesses what the corresponding printed results can establish. No solver was launched; no manuscript, figure, deck, parameter or existing audit file was changed.

## A. Paper summary

The authors use axisymmetric Sentaurus drift–diffusion simulations to compare a bare vertical β-Ga₂O₃ Schottky diode with specified planar p-GaN JTE, raised-ring JBS+JTE, and recessed-ring JBS+JTE designs. The study evaluates forward turn-on and resistance, reverse current thresholds on a common reference area, and sensitivity to Mg concentration, dimensions, Schottky barrier and temperature. At 298.15 K the nominal plated planar and raised-ring cases cross 1 mA/cm² near 2.336 kV, versus 1.133 kV for the tested recessed-ring case and 0.929 kV for the selected bare trace. At 400 K the planar and raised-ring designs differ substantially at 1 mA/cm², but their voltage brackets overlap at 10 mA/cm². The low-barrier 80 nm planar case illustrates a separate trade-off: ~0.355 V forward turn-on at 1 A/cm², but a reverse threshold of ~925 V at 0.1 mA/cm² versus ~2347 V at 1 mA/cm². The paper is a comparison of simulated devices, not a hardware demonstration or a search for absolute material breakdown limits.

## B. Strengths

1. **Meaningful technical question and coherent comparison.** The tested designs show why one reverse voltage is insufficient for comparing rectifiers. At 400 K the lower current target measures leakage behavior in the planar and raised-ring cases; the higher target samples the steep hole-majority branch. This is more useful than a peak-voltage ranking. The same practical issue appears in the thin, low-barrier case at room temperature.
2. **Headline values survive raw-data checks.** From the named cathode-current column, using the manuscript’s common area, corrected reverse voltage and five subsequent samples, the reviewer reproduced 928.657 V for the selected bare trace, 2335.902/2335.862/1133.433 V for the plated planar/mesa/trench, and 2346.862/924.653 V for the thin low-barrier case at the 1/0.1 mA/cm² thresholds. The nominal 150→250 nm result (629.080 V at 250 nm) and 1.5× Mg result (679.718 V) also reproduced. These are not compliance voltages. The two latter `.log_des.log` and `.stdout` files end during later Newton work, but the archived branches have 43 and 42 samples at or above the criterion, respectively; the threshold estimates are not the terminal failed Newton points.
3. **The temperature comparison has meaningful controls within each layout.** Nominal planar, mesa and trench model inputs were checked against the respective 350/400 K case decks. The parameter file and each family’s mesh are byte-identical across those cases; this supports interpreting the within-family differences as results for the specified temperature change. It does not make different architectures identical in contact area or mesh geometry.
4. **Several good limitations should be preserved.** The abstract and conclusion explicitly avoid fabrication tolerances and ratings. Fig. 4 is a limited crossing-scale benchmark, not a pre-knee fit. The caption of Fig. 5 explicitly identifies unequal saved external biases and scale saturation. The paper distinguishes BFOM from its study-specific turn-on metric and discloses Chang’s much higher reverse current criterion in Table III.

## C. Major concerns

### C1. A completed 80 nm / 1.10 eV run contradicts the unqualified Fig. 6 exclusion — confirmed

Section IV-B says the 80 nm case at nominal barrier did not establish a crossing. The attempted thickness-folder sweeps are incomplete, but the completed `NLM_g156e8_Full_Batch_20261122/10_Thin080nm_WF5p10/rev_10_Thin080nm_WF5p10.plt` crosses **2347.158 V at 1 mA/cm²**, and its run log explicitly reports cathode compliance, saved final output and normal exit. This is not merely a sibling with the same name: the 80 nm mesh and parameter files hash identically to those in `pGaN_thickness_doping/2B_Planar_t0p08um_RERUN/` (prefixes `d73423dff8e3212c` and `4290929042bd19e2`); the deck diff differs in paths/output naming and the rerun’s added initial plot, not in electrode, physics or sweep controls. Against nominal 150 nm case `03_Planar_WF5p10`, the substantive solver-deck change is the mesh, and the corresponding builders differ in `T_pgan` and output names. This resolves the earlier audits’ uncertainty about comparability at the prescribed settings, not about mesh convergence.

**Required:** Include the completed point or justify a specific exclusion policy and acknowledge the completed result. It changes which nominal-barrier thickness gives the highest sampled threshold and how the thin low-barrier device should be explained. An archive-based two-by-two comparison is available without new runs:

| Planar JTE | Input barrier | 0.1 mA/cm² crossing (V) | 1 mA/cm² crossing (V) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 150 nm | 1.10 eV | 2335.069 | 2335.902 |
| 80 nm | 1.10 eV | 2346.565 | 2347.158 |
| 150 nm | 0.75 eV | 926.146 | 2335.593 |
| 80 nm | 0.75 eV | 924.653 | 2346.862 |

At both thicknesses the stricter leakage crossing drops substantially when the input barrier is reduced; the 1 mA/cm² crossing remains near its own nominal-barrier value. The roughly 11 V difference between these meshes is an observed difference, not a mesh-independent thickness gain. The 150 nm / 0.75 eV forward trace also gives ~0.354 V, so the thin device should remain a selected operating point, not a claim of uniquely low turn-on or demonstrated global optimum. The narrower abstract statement “among the architecture cases” is accurate for Table II’s set.

### C2. Show the carrier evidence and stop assigning microscopic mechanisms from the wrong observable — overinterpretation, not a failed result

At 400 K, raw `temperature/{01_Planar,03_Mesa}_T400K/rev.plt` gives 1 mA/cm² crossings of **1472.976/2006.527 V**. On the upper sample of each bracket the signed collected-hole fraction is only **0.068%/0.544%**, respectively. At 10 mA/cm², the brackets are **2540.109–2555.596 V** and **2550.813–2551.233 V**. Hole fraction goes **2.49→99.49%** across the planar bracket and **64.45→84.04%** across the mesa bracket. The sampled current subsequently stays above each specified threshold; there is no resolved 6 V ordering of the high-current transition. At 350 K the planar 1 mA/cm² crossing also falls in a wide bracket, **2430.149–2446.026 V**, spanning a jump from approximately 0.01 to 506 mA/cm². Do not give the interpolated value or temperature slope more mechanistic precision than this sampling permits.

**Required:** Put the carrier fractions and raw brackets in IV-D or Fig. 10 (a small table suffices). Use a steep, sustained rise and hole majority as the project’s **simulated** knee diagnostic; the evidence distinguishes this from an early electron-leakage crossing. A terminal electron component cannot distinguish thermionic emission from field-assisted tunnelling; both are included in the model. The related fabricated study by Chang et al. (2025, DOI `10.1063/5.0243637`) and Li et al. (2020, DOI `10.1063/5.0029348`) provide context, not a mechanism identification for this deck. Likewise, Fig. 8’s unequal-bias fields and overlapping equilibrium bands do not, by themselves, prove that charge balance *causes* the observed threshold changes. State that the field redistribution is consistent with that interpretation. If the archived spatial results are discussed, distinguish the saved post-rise hotspot from the onset position. No new avalanche test, alternative breakdown criterion or experiment is required for the narrower conclusion.

### C3. Reconcile compared geometry and disclose the parameter provenance that matters — confirmed discrepancies and model limitations

- **Contact area:** Integrating the flat y=0 anode edges adjacent to epi Ga₂O₃ in the nominal meshes gives **8.2613×10⁻⁵ cm²** for planar and **4.6484×10⁻⁵ cm²** for both ring designs, or **56.27%**, not the stated **54.2%**. Calculation: intersect named anode line elements with epi triangle edges, deduplicate, and sum the swept annular areas `π|r₂²−r₁²|` (µm² converted to cm²); exclude curved/sidewall segments. If a different exposure convention is intended, show which intervals it excludes. The area correction does **not** change Table II’s common-`Aref` quantities. At comparable ~−2.003 kV bias the trend of greater mesa leakage per flat exposed area survives; a smaller area is consistent with lower total leakage, not proof of the sole cause.
- **Bare versus terminated:** Table II implies different substrates, but the checked builders use the same nominal `nTinActiveConcentration=3.6×10¹⁸ cm⁻³`; corresponding TDRs show the same nominal and ionized donor medians. The actual differences include lateral domain, contact geometry/split, interface scope, reverse numerics and series-resistance treatment. Do not imply an isolated termination gain or attribute the resistance difference to substrate doping. The original Yang paper (DOI `10.1063/1.4983203`) reports a **Hall carrier concentration**, whereas the deck enters that number as a donor concentration with incomplete ionization. That is a reference-model limitation, not a contradiction between the nominal bare and plated donor decks. The Yang benchmark already correctly disclaims matching pre-knee leakage.
- **Model inputs:** In §II-A, call εr=12.4 an isotropic model assignment based on the measured **(001)-normal** value, not the direct lattice c-axis value (Fiedler et al., DOI `10.1149/2.0201907jss`). Attribute the specific theoretical electron x-direction fit to Ghosh and Singisetti, Table I (DOI `10.1063/1.5034120`); identify the β-Ga₂O₃ hole terms as modeled inputs, not from that electron study. Source the GaN pair or identify the chosen combined model explicitly as an assumption with bulk-GaN precedents, not a calibrated property of sputtered material. Disclose the empirical tunnelling settings and fixed interface charge as such. The studied plated reverse decks include the artificial generation seed; `Bare/rev_test.cmd` does not. State the difference in II-A. Do not change coefficients merely to make a preferred curve match.

**Required:** Correct the record and add a short reproducibility note listing representative geometry/mesh/deck/parameter files, implemented temperatures, voltage reference, terminal current, compliance versus extraction threshold, and sample sustain rule. Give enough information to reproduce the comparison from the existing decks and traces, not just the summary plot. None of these corrections requires a new parameter sweep.

### C4. The Introduction establishes relevance, but not yet the right research gap — positioning issue

The p-GaN/β-Ga₂O₃ junction architecture, use of field-plated p-type III-nitride edge structures, and sweeps of geometry or temperature have direct precedents. The minimum scholarly comparison should discuss Nandi et al. (DOI `10.1109/TED.2021.3119261`), Than et al. (DOI `10.1088/1402-4896/ad6da2`), Roy et al. (DOI `10.1109/TED.2020.3025268`; author preprint `arXiv:2006.15645`), and Chang et al. [4,5]. Review 04 documents inspection depth and the limits of the structural comparisons. Chang’s measured 3 kV at **1 A/cm² reverse** is at 1000× the manuscript’s nominal 1 mA/cm² target; it is context, not validation of a 2.3 kV rating or a comparable performance ranking.

**Required:** State the gap as a comparison of these specified planar/raised/recessed layouts with a common extraction framework, emphasizing the **carrier-resolved, criterion-dependent result at 400 K**, and the low-barrier design’s stricter leakage penalty. The sampled thickness/dose results add design evidence, not a new termination principle. No first-of-kind claim is necessary for a defensible conference contribution. No experiment is requested for this comparative simulation scope.

## E. Questions for the authors

1. What selection rule led to excluding the completed 80 nm / 1.10 eV run from the nominal-barrier thickness comparison? If no physical exclusion is intended, will it be included and clearly separated from the 0.75 eV case?
2. Can IV-D show the collected-hole fractions and raw crossing brackets behind Fig. 10, and distinguish an electron-leakage threshold from the steep hole-current region without inferring the electron emission mechanism from terminal partition alone?
3. Which flat Schottky intervals and surfaces were counted in the published 54.2%, and which matched bias and current component were used in the local leakage comparison?
4. What is the provenance of the combined GaN ionization pair and the β-Ga₂O₃ hole fit, and which scope limits are intended for the exact voltages quoted?
5. Can the authors supply a minimal case-to-file and postprocessing manifest for the main comparisons, with the same-criterion numerical data separated from the literature device ratings?

## G. Overall recommendation — Major Revision

The paper addresses a useful rectifier-design problem and has enough numerical evidence for a focused conference contribution. I would not reject it for incremental architecture changes or absence of fabrication, because the multi-criterion result can stand as a comparative study. I also would not accept the current version as a minor copyedit: the available 80 nm nominal-barrier result changes the documented comparison set, and the temperature interpretation is the central novelty but is not tied to the carrier evidence in the submitted presentation. Correcting those two points, making the parameter and area assumptions explicit, and strengthening the prior-art comparison would address the substantive objections without expanding the study.
